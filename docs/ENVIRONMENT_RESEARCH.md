# Environment Research — Lower East Side Dive-Bar Pass

## Research conclusion

The useful target is not a themed room with distressed props. It is a narrow
ground-floor commercial space that has been repeatedly adapted inside an older
tenement shell. Authenticity comes from accumulated decisions: original
masonry and pressed metal, later plumbing and conduit, a patched concrete
floor, furniture acquired at different times, and small objects retained
because they are useful, sentimental, or simply never removed.

The scene is fictional. It does not copy the plan, signage, branding, or
protected artwork of any real or animated bar.

## Lower East Side construction evidence

- The [Tenement Museum's history of 97 Orchard Street](https://www.tenement.org/blog/five-stories-and-counting-the-history-of-97-orchard-street-in-five-terms/)
  records a typical 25 × 88.6 ft lot, a building occupying 68 ft of that depth,
  and a rear yard that historically served the saloon and apartments. The
  project's 21 ft 6 in clear interior width is therefore credible inside a
  roughly 25 ft masonry lot after wall thickness is allowed.
- The [East Village/Lower East Side Historic District report](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/2491.pdf)
  documents the neighborhood's progression from row houses to converted
  multiple dwellings and purpose-built tenements. A related [NYC Landmarks
  record](https://www.nyc.gov/site/lpc/about/pr2016/12-13.2.page) notes that
  storefronts were frequently added to residential lower stories as immigrant
  neighborhoods became mixed-use commercial districts.
- [97 Orchard Street](https://www.tenement.org/explore/97-orchard-street/)
  contained a lager beer saloon connected to the operators' adjoining living
  quarters. Museum descriptions show a long dark bar, lamps and trinkets,
  circular tables with multiple seats, and a room serving as a social gathering
  place rather than a single-purpose drinking machine.
- The Tenement Museum's [Shop Life reconstruction notes](https://www.tenement.org/blog/the-official-launch-of-shop-life/)
  emphasize paint selected from physical layers found on site and tiny working
  details. That supports a layered evidence strategy instead of a uniform
  procedural grunge pass; it does not require this functioning bar to display
  food waste or dirty customer surfaces.
- [McSorley's official tour](https://mcsorleysoldalehouse.nyc/a-tour-of-mcsorleys/)
  foregrounds retained room artifacts and long continuity rather than a newly
  themed vintage fit-out. That supports dense, place-specific history while the
  modeled signs, layout and objects remain fictional.
- [City Lore's Milano's Bar record](https://citylore.org/places/milanos-bar/)
  describes an old Bowery bar whose history survives in layers on its walls.
  Contemporary visual evidence of long, narrow East Side bars also supports a
  bar running along one side with remaining furniture compressed into the
  opposite perimeter and rear.

## What makes the room read as a dive bar

A dive bar is not defined by maximum dirt or by purchasing a matching set of
"vintage" props. The useful repeated traits in the field evidence are:

- **A constrained inherited shell.** Long, narrow circulation and furniture
  fitted around a pre-existing storefront make the room feel adapted rather
  than designed from a blank hospitality plan.
- **Acquisition over time.** Fixed booth construction may align, but stools,
  chairs, tables, lights, repairs, and retained equipment should reveal
  different purchase dates and budgets.
- **Operational density.** Bottles, register, taps, ice, soda gun, garnish,
  sink, drainboard, speed rail, waste, cooler, napkins, glasses, receipts, and
  tips occupy bartender reach zones. Random clutter cannot substitute for a
  plausible work sequence or a real aisle between opposing work surfaces.
- **Social memory.** Photos, flyers, stickers, team history, coats, notices,
  and old wiring accumulate at inconsistent heights. In this room most paper
  has compressed nearly flush to the plaster through paste, repainting and time;
  functional fixtures and wiring retain real mechanical supports.
- **Localized light.** The pool bed, bar work zone, booths, and exit path need
  different pools of light. A dark room with no readable source is unfinished,
  while uniform luxury-hotel fill erases the dive-bar hierarchy.

A detailed [Milano's field review](https://scoundrelsfieldguide.com/new-york/new-york-city/milanos-bar/)
describes a tiny shotgun space, a long bar consuming roughly half the room,
assorted low seating, a jukebox/TV compressed into the plan, a tin ceiling,
photo collages, string lights, and old shelves/bottles. The scene uses those as
general evidence of accumulation, not as a plan to copy.

## Clean versus worn

The target is neglected fabric, not unsafe housekeeping. A real working bar may
have ancient walls, chipped concrete, tired vinyl and decades of finish loss
while staff still wipe the bar and patron tables every shift. The scene therefore
separates these two states:

- floors and walls carry old cracks, patches, dry discoloration, scuffs and
  traffic polishing, but no fresh litter or rotting food;
- tabletops carry pale historical finish ghosts, small scratches and rounded
  edges beneath current clean coasters and served drinks, but no grime layer,
  loose caps, dirty abandoned glasses, food scraps or sticky puddles;
- the guest bar top stays open for service: three current drinks, one open tab,
  one dry away-place setting, a hollow tip jar, and an old enamel fruit bowl
  with whole fruit are deliberate shift objects rather than random debris;
- booth vinyl has fine cracking, tiny tears and narrow repairs, not broad decay;
- dense clutter is concentrated where work and memory produce it: bottle
  shelves, bartender modules, wall paper, coat hooks, wiring and utility zones.

## NYC booth references

The useful New York precedent is a small number of tight wall bays, not a
freestanding banquette run. The [Old Town Bar official site](https://oldtownbarnyc.com/)
shows compact dark-wood booth seating against the room perimeter. The
[Rudy's official history](https://rudysbarnyc.com/) documents an inherited
neighborhood-bar interior, while published interior photography in
[Blind Magazine](https://www.blind-magazine.com/news/a-toast-to-new-yorks-legendary-watering-holes/)
shows its small booth clusters and close clearances. These references support
the arrangement logic only; this project does not reproduce either interior.

The scene therefore uses two bays. Each has opposing benches running out from
the west wall, a table cleated at its short wall edge, and an open east mouth
where patrons enter from the pool aisle and slide inward. Patrons face each
other across the table. Upholstered backs define the bay edges without adding
false freestanding walls.

## Barware and the footprint of seated patrons

The glass envelopes come from current manufacturer pages rather than an
invented Blender scale. Libbey Foodservice lists its
[1639HT Restaurant Basics mixing glass](https://libbeyfoodservice.com/product/libbey/restaurant-basics-heat-treated-mixing-glass/1639ht)
as 16 oz, 3.5 in diameter and 5.88 in high. Libbey lists its
[15232 Gibraltar rocks glass](https://libbeyfoodservice.com/product/libbey/gibraltar-rocks/15232)
as 10 oz, 3.5 in diameter and 3.88 in high, with an eight-sided body. The scene
uses those exterior envelopes as dimensional references without reproducing a
logo or claiming a branded glass is installed.

A person leaves a reach pattern, not a showroom grid. Drinks are therefore
paired to explicit stool, booth-bench, or chair anchors; measured horizontal
reach is 0.244–0.656 m. Ten current drinks are distributed three at the bar,
four across the two booths, and three across the café tables. Fill levels,
straw directions and positions differ. Nine drinks use dry pulp coasters; one
pint sits directly on the bar with a fresh wipeable condensation halo. A dry
coaster and open tab at the fourth stool imply that patron has stepped to the
pool table. Reach is not the only constraint: every bar glass/coaster base is at
least 63 mm inside the guest edge and 216 mm inside the nearest end. This is
active tidy service, not abandoned dirty glassware.

## Hardware and bartender workflow

The front-door correction follows the assembly relationships shown for an
[Adams Rite 8400 narrow-stile mortise exit device](https://www.adamsrite.com/en/products/exit-devices/8400-series-life-safety-narrow-stile-mortise-exit-device):
a horizontal interior rail acts through a latch case in the stile. The modeled
leaf adds a jamb strike, hinges, threshold, and connected closer. This choice
is more credible than a floating vertical pull, but the project does not claim
that a particular panic device is legally required for this fictional occupant
load.

NYC's [2022 Building Code Chapter 10](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/buildings/apps/pdf_viewer/viewer.html?file=2022BC_Chapter10_EgressWBwm.pdf&section=conscode_2022)
requires illumination in occupied exit access. That supports a visible entry
globe and illuminated sign instead of a black door corner. It does not make
this visualization a code review.

For the bartender side, manufacturer workstation layouts consistently combine
the same modules. Glastender's
[ALL-66C specification](https://www.glastender.com/bar-fabrication/signature/all-in-one-stations/all-66c)
lists an ice bin, recessed drainboard, wet-waste sink, towel ring, speed rail,
dry waste and condiment dispenser. Krowne's
[underbar system overview](https://krowne.com/product-showcase/underbar) likewise
separates ice bins, drainboards, trash, glass washing, soda-gun holders and
speed rails, while Perlick's
[underbar design guide](https://www.perlick.com/pub/media//akeneo_connector/media_files/T/o/Top_Shelf_Stainless_Steel_Underbar_Design_Guide_lo_res_433b.pdf)
shows those modules organized around a bartender aisle. These are functional
references, not a mandate to make the dive bar look newly installed.
Historical bar imagery in the
[Smithsonian collection](https://nmaahc.si.edu/object/nmaahc_2014.37.36.5)
also supports varied bottle stock with the register integrated into the back
bar. The resulting scene separates 193 supported back-stock bottles from eight
open working well bottles with pourers. A shallow backbar and service counter
leave a measured 703 mm aisle; scoured metal, mismatched modules, repairs and
old work clutter keep the workflow from reading as a new premium installation.

## Billiard-light decision

The [WPA Recommended Equipment Specifications](https://wpapool.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RECOMMENDED-EQUIPMENT-SPECIFICATIONS.pdf)
distinguish two conditions: a fixture that a referee can move aside may be as
low as 40 in above the bed; a non-movable fixture should be at least 65 in
above it. This venue uses the movable chain-hung condition requested by art
direction. With a 0.762 m bed, the shade bottom is 1.778 m above the floor—just
under 5 ft 10 in—and remains centered over a zone where patrons do not walk.

## Translation into this scene

- Existing 6.55 × 11.58 m public room is treated as the front commercial
  portion of a deeper tenement lot, not the whole building.
- Street end: separate framed glazed entrance and transom beside the diamond
  storefront window. The door occupies an actual wall opening.
- West/front: a 3.80 m working bar fully south of the cue rectangle, two-deep
  stock in seven bottle families, bartender-facing register, 703 mm service
  aisle, ice/well/soda/garnish, sink/drainboard/waste, taps/cooler, and decades
  of useful or sentimental junk.
- East/front: two small cafe tables with visibly mismatched chairs and a clear
  central aisle from the entrance; three current drinks align to chair reach.
- West/rear: two face-to-face field-built booth bays entered from the pool side
  and a memory wall, all outside the nominal cue envelope; four current drinks
  sit in opposing bench reach zones.
- Rear: separate service and restroom doors set into real masonry openings.
  They imply additional service depth beyond the rendered public room.
- Floor: later exposed concrete retrofit with shrinkage cracks, authored
  fractures, aggregate patches, dry mineral/rust shadows, old sealed-in drink
  discoloration and traffic-polished paths; swept, with no litter or fresh grime.
- Walls: original generated period-art and irregular paper-history assets sit
  almost flush like old wheat-paste; notes, coats, a dense flat restroom-door
  sticker field and pool equipment occupy different heights and ages without
  picture-frame shadows.
- Street: an original wet-night LES plate sits beyond the diamond storefront,
  with blurred cars, fictional small-business glow and multiple modeled color
  spills. It is atmospheric evidence, not a copy of a real block.

This is a constructionally plausible visualization, not a permit set. It does
not assert current NYC egress, accessibility, plumbing, occupancy, fire, or
health-code compliance.
